# Decision828 L2 ablation

This is the single frozen follow-up to `DECISION828_CONTEXT_ANALYSIS.md`. It
starts from the natural B state immediately after conflict 657 and before the
native decision 828 pick. At that boundary the L2 clause `[-507, 565, 566]`
has no active reason, all three literals are unassigned, and its two watches
are present. The intervention strictly detaches and frees that one original
clause, records a DRAT deletion, and leaves the assignment, trail, reasons,
activity, heap, phases, restart queues, learned database, and native pick
untouched.

The control rerun is byte-identical to frozen B (169,858 analysis operations,
4,544 conflicts, 5,286 decisions, 359,859 propagations). The ablated run is
still UNSAT and its proof is independently verified by `drat-trim`:

| run | analysis ops | conflicts | decisions | propagations |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B | 169,858 | 4,544 | 5,286 | 359,859 |
| B with L2 removed at d828 | 174,427 | 4,698 | 5,421 | 367,157 |

Removing L2 increases analysis work by 4,569 operations (+2.69%) and does not
destroy the short trajectory. Therefore the context formed before decision828
is sufficient for most of B's advantage in this intervention. Continued L2
availability after d828 still has a measurable contribution, but it is not the
whole explanation and is not required for the 169k-scale trajectory.

This does not identify a sufficient context state: it ablates one clause only,
and the resulting watch/database difference is intentional. It also does not
show that every late L2 use is dispensable. The exact source hashes, boundary
snapshot equality, deletion guards, event trace, proof, and checker output are
in [results/decision828_l2_remove](results/decision828_l2_remove), generated by
[decision828_l2_remove.py](decision828_l2_remove.py).
